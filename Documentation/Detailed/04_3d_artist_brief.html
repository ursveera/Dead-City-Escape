2. Art Direction

Core Visual Pillars

  • Recent Collapse: The city should feel like society collapsed days or weeks ago, not hundreds of years ago.
  • Wet and Reflective: Rain, water puddles, damp concrete, wet asphalt, and shiny blood surfaces are important.
  • Readable Horror: Darkness is allowed, but silhouettes, interactable objects, doors, paths, and enemies must remain readable.
  • Cinematic Realism: Believable proportions, grounded materials, practical lighting sources, and restrained color use.
  • Co-op Readability: Two players must identify each other quickly in dark environments.

3. World Storytelling Rules

Every area must answer: who lived here, what happened, how did people try to survive, and where did things go wrong?

Story ElementExamplesArtist Goal
Evacuation FailureSuitcases, barricades, abandoned cars, military signsShow panic and blocked escape routes
Medical CollapseBody bags, broken monitors, blood trails, emergency triage tentsShow the infection overwhelmed the system
LootingBroken glass, empty shelves, scattered boxesShow desperation without cluttering gameplay paths
Survivor PresenceCampfire remains, handwritten warnings, boarded doorsSuggest other survivors existed before the players arrived
Combat HistoryBullet holes, blood splashes, broken weapons, shell casingsShow past fights and guide player attention

Required AssetsWeapon racks, broken glass, ammo crates, counter, security shutters, neon sign, alarm light

PurposeFinal two-player horde defense

7. Technical Art Standards

StandardRequirement
Scale1 Unity unit = 1 meter
Default Character HeightMale survivor 1.8m, female survivor 1.7m, standard zombie 1.75m, tank 2.6m
TransformsFreeze/apply transforms before export
TopologyGame-ready quads preferred during creation; triangulated result acceptable on export
NormalsClean normals, weighted normals where useful, no broken smoothing
UVsNo overlapping UVs unless intentionally mirrored; second UV channel for lightmaps when needed
RigUnity Humanoid compatible for survivors and standard zombies where possible
SocketsWeapons and interactive props need sockets/empties clearly named

8. Modular Kit Rules

  • Snap modular environment assets to a 1m grid.
  • Use 2m and 4m standard wall lengths whenever possible.
  • Keep pivots consistent: walls bottom-center, doors at hinge, props bottom-center.
  • Separate modular structure from set dressing props.
  • Avoid creating one huge environment mesh. Build reusable pieces.

Collision Rules

  • Use simple primitive collisions for most props.
  • Create custom low-poly collision meshes for complex hero assets.
  • Avoid mesh colliders unless explicitly approved.
  • Collision mesh naming: UCX_AssetName_01 or COL_AssetName_01.
  • Do not add collision to tiny decorative clutter unless gameplay requires it.

Export Checklist

  • FBX format unless otherwise approved.
  • Correct scale in Unity.
  • Transforms applied/frozen.
  • Pivot placed correctly.
  • No unused meshes, cameras, lights, or hidden objects in export.
  • Textures named correctly and linked.
  • LOD meshes exported or packaged clearly.
  • Collision mesh included if required.
  • Preview screenshot included for review.

Art Director Review Checklist

  • Matches Dead City Escape tone and style.
  • Readable in dark, foggy, multiplayer scenes.
  • Scale is correct against 1.8m player reference.
  • Materials are believable and not too clean.
  • Asset has enough damage, grime, story detail, and variation.
  • Does not block gameplay visibility or co-op movement.
  • Optimized for real-time Unity gameplay.

Technical Artist Review Checklist

  • Correct naming and folder placement.
  • Clean mesh, normals, UVs, and pivots.
  • Texture sizes fit budget.
  • LODs and collision are correct.
  • No excessive materials or draw calls.
  • Imports cleanly into Unity with no scale or rotation issues.
Final Quality Target: Assets should feel production-ready for a professional indie or AA game vertical slice. They do not need AAA budget complexity, but they must follow AAA-style discipline: clear naming, correct scale, reusable modularity, optimized geometry, readable silhouettes, and strong environmental storytelling.
